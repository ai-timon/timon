1. Open Timon. On the very first launch, the window opens in the colours of the welcome screen and stays quiet until it is ready (a small spinner appears if it takes a while). On later launches, the desktop app shows a **Starting Timon…** screen with the current step while it starts. If the interface never answers, the screen says so. When there is no configuration file, the setup screen checks the machine and presents the initial configuration flow. On a fresh install, a welcome screen comes first. These screens follow your system's light or dark mode; the switch in the top-right corner changes it at once, and the account you create keeps that choice. Pick your language on the roller, each shown with its flag (the screen switches as soon as the roller stops on it, and the next screens follow), then click **Continue**. **Create your local account** comes next, on one screen: your first name (optional — your agents call you by it), your email and a password typed twice (at least 8 characters, nothing more required). **Create my account** emails a 6-digit code to that address: type it and the account is created. **Resend** opens after 60 seconds, and **Change email** brings back the form as you filled it. This needs an internet connection. Timon only collects your email, in case you want to reset your password; the account you create stays local. If the web interface is reachable from the network (a server, a VPS), the form also asks for a one-time *Setup token*: run `timon setup-token` on that machine and paste the result.
2. Install missing required tools and authenticate your chosen CLI backend. If an action requires elevated rights, setup gives you the command to run yourself. When a CLI backend (Claude, Codex, Grok or Copilot) already works on your machine, setup does not show the backend list at all. Installed a tool yourself in a terminal? Click **Re-detect** in the **Prerequisites** panel — no need to close the window or restart Timon; setup also checks again when you switch back to its window, and reloading the page keeps the paths and choices you had entered.
3. Timon installs its background service on its own while you create your account, choose your AI and your profile. A **Preparing Timon** card in the bottom-right corner follows it: background service, local database, built-in browser, and the search for your AIs; it ends on **Timon is ready**. Windows asks for administrator approval and macOS may ask for your password while this happens: accept to let Timon keep working when its window is closed. If the service is refused, the card says why, gives the command to run yourself when there is one, and offers **Try again** and **Open the repair screen** (the former setup screen, where the service can also be installed with a button); the account, AI and profile steps keep working meanwhile. Creating your agent waits for the service: in the desktop app, Timon then briefly shows **Restarting Timon…** while it moves onto the service, and continues with your main agent. Linux without systemd runs in the labelled portable mode: Timon works while the app is open.
4. Save the initial configuration. Free runs without a licence key, registration or activation, and includes every feature and plugin for one account, except replacing the whole interface with a third-party layout (White Label), which needs a licence that includes it. Creating your account asks for no key: enter one later in **Settings › Profile › License key**, only when you need additional user accounts.
5. Open **Agents**, select an available agent and send a small request in Chat, such as asking it to describe the files in its working directory.

On a server without a display, create the account from the terminal with `timon init` instead, then open the web interface to continue from the next step: see [Run Timon on a headless server](headless-server.md#2-create-your-account-first).

Right after your account, the **Choose your LLM** screen offers two ways to run your agents. **Use my AI subscription**, which names the AI tools this version of Timon supports, opens them on the same screen, as described below; **Ready with …** names the ones Timon can start on, and **Continue anyway** disappears as soon as one is there. **Use local models** shows Ollama: download it from its site, then add it as a provider in Settings › AI models › Providers. **Back** returns to the two choices first. Usage statistics are not asked here: they are on by default and turned off in Settings › Privacy.

Next, **What brings you here?** asks your profile in one click: **I write code** (agents that code, review and merge in your repositories), **I automate** (email, calendar, watch, routines that run on their own) or **I'm discovering** (AI agents, step by step). Timon tunes its defaults to your answer; **Back** returns to the previous screen and shows your choice, and a click on another row changes it.

Then **Bring your agent to life** creates your main agent. On the left, its card: a mascot that dresses for its job until you style it, **Random** for a whole new look, **Customise** for a side panel where you pick its skull, fur, eyes and accessory (with a live preview and states to try; **Done** or Escape closes it), its name with a button that draws one, and its role and model. On the right, the view your profile recommends, starred in the switcher at the top right that opens the two others: **Templates** (six ready-made profiles such as a personal assistant or a coding partner), **Interview** (five questions: what to automate, which tools, when to start, what to do before an important action, where to let you know; every answer stays editable), or **Sheet** (role, project folder, model from the AIs you connected, exact permission mode — `bypassPermissions` warns that no approval card will ever show — skills, MCP servers and instructions). The button under the view creates the agent with all of it.

Then **Discover Timon** plays four short animated slides, starring the TIM you just made: your TIMs work on their own, they form teams, projects have their own TIMs that work in that project only, with its rules and its memory, and connectors, workflows and plugins plug in around them. Each slide names the views where you will find it. A slide moves on by itself after a few seconds (resting the pointer on it holds it), **Next** or the → key goes on at once, ← goes back, a bar at the top jumps to its slide, and **Skip all** leaves straight away. The last button, **Meet \{name\}**, opens the **TIMs** page, where your agent welcomes you (below). Everything stays editable later from the agent's page.

On the subscription choice (or the **Your AIs** step when setup opens it directly), Timon shows one row per AI tool Timon works with (Claude Code, Codex, Grok, Copilot CLI, opencode, Hermes, Pi) and its state on this computer: not installed, installed but not signed in, ready. **Install** runs the vendor's official installer for you — no Node.js needed — and shows the installer's progress while it works; if it fails, the row gives the same command to paste into a terminal. A tool with no installer Timon can run on your system links to its vendor's installation page. Once installed, **Sign in** copies the tool's sign-in command (or types it in Timon's terminal when one is open): paste it into a new terminal, press Enter, sign in through your browser, then come back to Timon: it checks again on its own (or click **Try again**). **Continue** becomes available as soon as Claude, Codex, Grok or Copilot is installed and not signed out; **Continue anyway** moves on regardless. The same cards are in Settings › AI models › Providers, where they also offer updates (see [Settings](../usage/settings.md#ai-tools-on-this-machine)).

Once created, your main agent — your first TIM — welcomes you on the **TIMs** page: the list of your TIMs on the left, its conversation on the right, already open. It speaks first, in the language you picked on the welcome screen. It does not ask again what you just told Timon (your language, its name and look, your profile): it writes its character (its *SOUL*) from the card you made, then asks **Hey \{your first name\}, what do you want to do now?** Its questions arrive as cards: click an option, or type your answer.

- **Create another TIM.** It explains what a TIM is, asks what the new one should do, then points you to the **New TIM** button under the list, with a name and a job you can type into the editor.
- **Create a project with a team of TIMs.** It explains what a project is — a folder on your computer, a team of TIMs that work in it, tasks on the Kanban board — and how the **Agents** page draws the team as a canvas of who reports to whom. It then offers to register one of your folders as a project with a coder and a reviewer, or to create a small demo project; it creates nothing before your yes.
- **Set up what its card asked.** Offered only when its card asks for something at a set time (every day at 08:00) or on an event (a webhook). What it creates is saved switched off: switch it on in the **Triggers** page, in the **Routines** tab for something at a set time, in the **Webhooks** tab for a webhook (see [Triggers](../usage/triggers.md)). On every new email, or for news on Telegram or by email, it tells you which connector to add in Settings first.

After your choice the rest of Timon opens, and your main agent stays in the same conversation: keep talking to it. Until then, other pages bring you back to this welcome. If you reload the page or come back later, the same conversation is still there. If Timon still has to restart to run on the new configuration, a banner at the top of the window offers the restart. The agent works in the folder its card names: the one you picked on the **Sheet**, otherwise a folder Timon creates for it. You can change its name, look, picture and instructions later from its page.

If its backend fails, Timon tries the next installed one (Claude, then Codex, Grok, Copilot). If none answers, the TIMs page shows the AI tool cards instead of the conversation so you can install or sign in to one, then click **Try again**. On macOS, when the CLI only comes with the Claude or ChatGPT desktop app, the card reads **Desktop app — sign in**: nothing needs installing, the card gives the line that signs in the copy the app ships (for Claude, type `/login` if it asks), and the standalone installer stays offered as an alternative.

**Skip the welcome**, just above the conversation, opens the rest of Timon at once: your main agent keeps the card you made, and its character can be written later from its page. When the conversation runs into trouble — its tools are refused, it is interrupted or stops, or the agent says nothing — a card takes its place, says what happened and puts **Skip the welcome** forward; when the conversation has stopped, it also offers **Restart the conversation**. If the TIMs page is not available on your install, the same welcome opens on the setup's repair screen instead.

Watch the mission's activity and final response. For a custom agent, choose a real project directory and the backend/model from the available catalogue; see [Agents](../usage/agents.md).

## Confirm the setup

```bash
timon service status
timon doctor
timon backends
timon list
```

The first two diagnose installation; the latter two inspect available backends and agents. Match the CLI's target address to the instance you opened in the browser. See [Command-line interface](../usage/cli.md).

If the conversation does not start, inspect the mission's error before trying again. A missing CLI, missing authentication and an invalid working directory require different fixes; [Troubleshooting](troubleshooting.md) covers each.

To add user accounts, you need a pro licence and the Multi-user plugin: see [Several accounts](../usage/multi-user.md). Free does not require this step.
